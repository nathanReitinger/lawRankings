Law School Rankings

Rankings About & method

What is this site?

This site provides rankings and analytics on law school faculty scholarship output. This metric-based sorting of schools helps bring transparency to publications and helps connect communities of scholars. It is modeled on CSrankings.

How are rankings calculated?

Just as they are on CSrankings. Each article is one credit, split evenly between its co-authors (the adjusted count). Every group you select is one area: the flagship law reviews (all the US News tiers you check, together), and each specialty group you check. A school's count is the geometric mean, across the selected areas, of its faculty's total adjusted count in that area plus one. Articles are credited to the school where the author teaches now, so a professor who moves brings their earlier articles along.

What are "flagship" law reviews?

A school's main, general-interest law review, such as the Harvard Law Review or the Northwestern University Law Review. W&L lists these in its "General" category, together with online editions (the Yale Law Journal Forum, for example) and a few other general journals; in the rankings those form their own tier, "Online editions & other general journals", which is off by default. The other journals are specialty journals, which this site groups into nine areas. Open a faculty member on the rankings page to see bars for where they publish: one for flagship law reviews and one for each specialty group they have published in, counting all their articles in the years you chose (articles outside the selected journals are listed in gray).

How are journals ranked?

Each kind of journal has one fixed source, and the site offers no other method:

  • Flagship law reviews: U.S. News. A school's main law review takes the U.S. News rank of that school (). The tiers on the rankings page ("US News 1–3", "4–25", "26–50", "51 and above") are these ranks; schools tied at a rank share a tier, and reviews of schools US News leaves unranked are in "51 and above". The default view counts the main law reviews of the top 25.
  • Specialty journals: Washington & Lee. A specialty journal takes its rank in the Washington & Lee Law Journal Rankings (, combined score, US journals). A specialty group is re-ranked by W&L score among the subjects you leave checked, and "top 10" counts that group's ten highest-scoring journals.

These ranks only decide which journals are counted. Schools themselves are ordered by the count described above.
