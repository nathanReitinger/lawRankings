Rankings About & method

What is this site?

This site provides rankings and analytics on law school faculty scholarship output. This metric-based sorting of schools helps bring transparency to publications and helps connect communities of scholars. It is modeled on CSrankings.

How are rankings calculated?

Just as they are on CSrankings. Each article is one credit, split evenly between its co-authors (the adjusted count). Every group you select is one area: the General law reviews (all the W&L rank bands you check, together), and each specialty group you check. A school's count is the geometric mean, across the selected areas, of its faculty's total adjusted count in that area plus one. Articles are credited to the school where the author teaches now, so a professor who moves brings their earlier articles along.

Where do journal ranks come from?

The Washington & Lee Law Journal Rankings (), combined score, US journals. General law reviews use W&L's General rank. A specialty group is re-ranked by W&L score among the subjects you leave checked, and "top 10" counts that group's ten highest-scoring journals.

    Where do the articles and authors come from?

    HeinOnline metadata. Articles come from each journal's table of contents on HeinOnline; faculty come from HeinOnline's list of authors by school. An article's author is matched to a faculty member by name (first and last name, using the middle name to tell people apart), so people with very common names can be missed or, rarely, mismatched.

    Which schools are US law schools comes from the US News law school list.

    What counts as a "short piece"?

    For now, a title-based guess: forewords, introductions, tributes, in memoriams, book reviews, remarks, panels, transcripts, and responses or replies. "Skip short pieces" drops these. Page counts would be a better test; the update script now saves them, so this will improve.
