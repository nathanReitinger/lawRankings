Law School Rankings: FAQs

What is this site?

This site provides rankings and analytics on law school faculty scholarship output. This metric-based sorting of schools helps bring transparency to publications and helps connect communities of scholars.

How are rankings calculated?

Rankings are calculated just as they are with CSRankings.org. Each paper is one credit, split evenly between its co-authors (the adjusted count). Each category you select (a General tier, a specialty group, or a single W&L subject) is one area; a school's score is the geometric mean, across the selected areas, of its faculty's total adjusted count plus one. Papers are credited to the school where the author teaches now, so a professor who moved brings their earlier papers with them.

Where do journal ranks come from?

Ranks come from the Washington & Lee Law Journal Rankings (). "Top 10" means a journal's W&L rank. For the specialty groups, journals are re-ranked within the group by their W&L score.

What is in each specialty group?

The groups combine W&L's subject lists. Individual subjects are still available under "More" in the filters.

    Where do the papers and authors come from?

    HeinOnline metadata. Papers come from each journal's table of contents; faculty come from HeinOnline's list of authors by school. A paper's author is matched to a faculty member by name (first and last name, using the middle name when it helps tell people apart), so people with very common names can be missed or, rarely, mismatched.

    What counts as a "short piece"?

    For now, a title-based guess: forewords, introductions, tributes, in memoriams, book reviews, remarks, panels, transcripts, and responses or replies. Turning on "Skip short pieces" drops these. Page counts would be a better test and are planned.
