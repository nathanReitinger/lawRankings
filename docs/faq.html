Law School Rankings

Rankings About & method

What is this site?

This site provides rankings and analytics on law school faculty scholarship output. This metric-based sorting of schools helps bring transparency to publications and helps connect communities of scholars. It is modeled on CSrankings.

How are rankings calculated?

Just as they are on CSrankings. Each article is one credit, split evenly between its co-authors (the adjusted count). Every group you select is one area: the flagship law reviews (all the US News tiers you check, together), and each specialty group you check. A school's count is the geometric mean, across the selected areas, of its faculty's total adjusted count in that area plus one. Articles are credited to the school where the author teaches now, so a professor who moves brings their earlier articles along.

What are "flagship" law reviews?

A school's main, general-interest law review, such as the Harvard Law Review or the Northwestern University Law Review. W&L lists these in its "General" category, together with online editions (the Yale Law Journal Forum, for example) and a few other general journals; in the rankings those form their own tier, "Online editions & other general journals", which is off by default. The other journals are specialty journals, which this site groups into nine areas. Open a faculty member on the rankings page to see bars for where they publish: one for flagship law reviews and one for each specialty group they have published in, counting all their articles in the years you chose, with how many of them count in the current ranking. The site does not list individual articles; each name links to the person's HeinOnline author profile.

What do the tags next to a name mean?

Where that person publishes most among the specialty journals: the specialty group with the most of their specialty-journal articles, over all their articles and all years (an article in a journal that belongs to two groups counts half toward each), and a second group when it has at least half as many. People with fewer than two specialty-journal articles have no tag. Tags do not change with the filters; the bars in each person's drop-down show the full picture for the years you choose.

How are articles matched to faculty?

By HeinOnline's own author profiles. The faculty come from HeinOnline's lists of faculty by school, and each person there has one HeinOnline author profile, which names every spelling HeinOnline counts as that person (for one professor: "Huq, Aziz Z.", "Huq, Aziz", "Huq, A.Z."). An article counts for a faculty member when its author, as HeinOnline lists it in the journal's table of contents, is one of those spellings (or the same name with only punctuation or capitals changed). A spelling that appears in two people's profiles counts for the person whose own profile name it is, or for no one.

The profiles are read a few hundred a day, the least certain cases first (). Until a person's profile has been read, their articles are matched by name: the same last and first name, with middle names or initials that do not conflict. A name that belongs to two faculty members is credited to neither, and an article whose author has a middle initial is not credited to a faculty member listed without one unless at least 85% of the articles under that name that give a middle initial give that one. If something looks wrong, please suggest a correction.

How are journals ranked?

Each kind of journal has one fixed source, and the site offers no other method:

  • Flagship law reviews: U.S. News. A school's main law review takes the U.S. News rank of that school (). The tiers on the rankings page ("US News 1–3", "4–25", "26–50", "51 and above") are these ranks; schools tied at a rank share a tier, and reviews of schools US News leaves unranked are in "51 and above". The default view counts the main law reviews of the top 25.
  • Specialty journals: Washington & Lee. A specialty journal takes its rank in the Washington & Lee Law Journal Rankings (, combined score, US journals). A specialty group is re-ranked by W&L score among the subjects you leave checked, and "top 10" counts that group's ten highest-scoring journals.

These ranks only decide which journals are counted. Schools themselves are ordered by the count described above.

    What counts as a "short piece"? (beta)

    "Skip short pieces" leaves out a piece that runs under 10 pages, or a memorial or tribute under 20 pages. The length comes from page numbers in HeinOnline's metadata: the next entry's first page minus this one's, counting every entry in the volume's table of contents. Where a piece's length is not known yet, a trained classifier decides.     What do the version numbers mean?

    The volume number in the top left is a version, major.data.site. The middle number goes up whenever the substantive data changes (articles, faculty, schools, journals or their ranks; the site keeps a fingerprint of the data to tell), and the timestamp in the top right is the moment of that change, in Unix time. The last number goes up for changes to the site itself that leave the data alone. The first number goes up for a major update. The version history lists every version.
