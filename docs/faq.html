About this site

Rankings and analytics of law school faculty scholarship: law schools ordered by their current faculty's articles in selected law journals. The site is modeled on CSrankings.

Ranking calculation

The calculation follows CSrankings. Each article is one credit, split evenly between its co-authors (the adjusted count). Each area is either the flagship law reviews (the tiers checked under Flagship Law Reviews, combined) or one higher-level group of specialty journals (the groups checked in it, combined). A school's count is the geometric mean, across the selected areas, of its faculty's total adjusted count in that area plus one. Articles are credited to the school where the author teaches now; a professor's earlier articles count for their current school.

Options: "Per professor" and "Leave out a school's own journals"

Two settings under Options, both off by default and not part of the default ranking. Per professor divides each school's count (less the one added to each area) by the number of people on its faculty list; with one area, the result is adjusted articles per listed professor. The list is HeinOnline's faculty list for the school, which can include clinical, emeritus and visiting faculty; schools with fewer than ten people listed are left out. Leave out a school's own journals stops counting an article for the author's school when that school publishes the journal: its main law review, its online edition, or a specialty journal it hosts. The article still counts for a co-author at another school. Rank correlations of each option with U.S. News: see below.

Flagship law reviews and specialty journals

Flagship law reviews: a school's main, general-interest law review, such as the Harvard Law Review or the Northwestern University Law Review. W&L lists these in its "General" category, together with online editions (the Yale Law Journal Forum, for example) and a few other general journals; in the rankings those form their own tier, "Online editions & other general journals", which is off by default. The other journals are specialty journals. Each sits in one of 52 groups, and each group in one higher-level group: thirteen subject areas, plus "Interdisciplinary Methods" for the method journals and "General & Policy Journals" for journals whose scope is law or policy generally. Each journal is placed by its own statement of its scope and its W&L subject; see Subject areas for the tree, how it was built and its sources. A faculty member's row on the rankings page opens to bars by where they publish: one for flagship law reviews, one for online editions & other general journals, and one for each higher-level group they have published in, counting their articles in the selected years (without short pieces when "Skip short pieces" is on), with the number that count in the current ranking. The site does not list individual articles; a name links to the person's faculty page where one is on file.

Tags next to a name

A person's tags: the higher-level groups (subject areas) of the specialty journals they have published in, each with the number of their articles there, across the years in the data. Each specialty journal sits in one group and each group in one higher-level group; the Subject areas page shows each journal's group and higher-level group and the source of each placement (the journal's own statement of its scope and its W&L subject). A tag comes from the journal's placement; titles and thresholds are not used. Journals whose scope is law or policy generally make no tag. Articles in two areas' journals make two tags. A person with articles only in flagship law reviews has no tag. A faculty member's row opens to "Subject areas, from specialty journals": the same groups as a small tree (higher-level group, group, journals) for the selected years.

Examples, as of October 2026: Mark Lemley (Stanford) is tagged Science, Technology & IP 31 (technology law 16, intellectual property 11, internet, computing & communications law 2 and two more: the Berkeley Technology Law Journal, the Harvard Journal of Law & Technology, the AIPLA Quarterly Journal and others), and also Business & Financial Law 2, Constitutional & Public Law 1 and Interdisciplinary Methods 1; Richard Revesz (NYU), Property, Environment & Natural Resources 18 (environmental, energy & natural resources law, from the Environmental Law Reporter to the Harvard Environmental Law Review), Constitutional & Public Law 5 (administrative law & regulation) and Interdisciplinary Methods 5 (the Journal of Legal Studies); Stephen Henderson (Oklahoma), Criminal Law & Justice 9, Constitutional & Public Law 3 and Science, Technology & IP 1; Reuven Avi-Yonah (Michigan), Tax, Trusts & Estates 25, International & Comparative Law 9 and four more. A count of 1 is shown like other counts. The four also have articles in flagship law reviews, which make no tag.

By-year lines and charts

Each school and each faculty member in the ranking has a By year line (on screens wider than a phone): their counted articles in the selected journals in each selected year, oldest on the left, adjusted for co-authors and scaled to their own highest year; hover it for the numbers. A school's or person's row opens to a line chart of the same counts over the twenty years up to the end of the selected range, or the selected range when it is longer than twenty years (the selected years shaded), with one line per selected group of journals (when more than five are selected, the smaller ones are combined into one line) and one for their total. The charts end at the last full year (the year before the current one). The dashed trend line is a Theil–Sen line through the total over the years shown: its slope is the median of the slopes between each pair of years, so one unusual year moves it little. It was chosen over a least-squares line, Huber and least-absolute-deviation lines, Siegel's repeated medians and a Poisson curve in a test on the site's yearly counts for 2007–2025 (each fitted on earlier years and scored on the next one to three, and refitted with each year left out); it is not drawn past the last full year. The lines and charts follow the filters for journals, years, short pieces and "Leave out a school's own journals"; "Per professor" changes only the ranking, and the lines stay counts.

A school also has subject areas, read the same way as a person's tags: the higher-level groups of the specialty journals its current faculty published in during the selected years, each with its number of articles (an article two of its faculty wrote counts once). The top three show beside its name on wider screens; the school's opened row has bars of its faculty's articles by kind of journal and group.

Beta: categories from titles

A setting under Options ("Beta: categories from titles"), off by default; it changes the tags only when switched on. A model assigns an article a higher-level group from its title: TF-IDF word features and a logistic regression, trained on 181,196 titles of articles in 209 specialty journals, each labeled with its journal's higher-level group. Accuracy on held-out journals (five folds, grouped by journal): 46% of titles, and 71% with the correct group among its top three; assigning the largest group to each title: 18%. Accuracy by higher-level group includes international law 72%, science, technology & IP 64%, courts and litigation 20%, labor 20% and legal theory 2%. With the switch on, a person's tags include articles in the collected journals, flagship law reviews included, whose title the model assigns to a group with probability 50% or higher, marked β. The default tags come from specialty journals only.

Matching articles to faculty

Articles are matched to faculty by name, against HeinOnline's lists of faculty by school, where each person appears once under their HeinOnline author name. An article counts for a faculty member when its author, as the journal's table of contents lists it, has the same last and first name, with middle names or initials that do not conflict (the exact name first; then the first and last name when only one person on the lists has it; then the same ignoring a "Jr." or "III" one side leaves off). A name that belongs to two faculty members is credited to neither; an article whose author has a middle initial is not credited to a faculty member listed without one unless at least 85% of the articles under that name that give a middle initial give that one; and in magazines that are not a school's journal (the ABA Journal, for example), an author listed without a middle initial is not credited to a faculty member listed with one. A second pass then catches the other ways one person's name is written, each only when exactly one person on the lists fits: the same words with the comma elsewhere ("Reiser, Dana Brakman" for "Brakman Reiser, Dana"), an initial plus the name someone goes by ("R. George Wright" for Richard George Wright), initials only ("J. M. Balkin"), a title before the name ("Dean Matthew Diller"), and a string that lost its comma. Initials count only for papers from 1985 on and within ten years of the person's other work. A nickname ("Rich" for Richard), the middle name used as the first, or a middle initial the list leaves off counts only with evidence: a paper in a journal the person's own school publishes, or a co-author shared with their other papers. A name HeinOnline itself marks as a different person ("Gardner, James A. (I)") is not credited to the untagged name. A piece HeinOnline labels a student Note is not credited to a faculty member whose other credited work began five or more years before it; an editor's, research or historical note is credited to the faculty member, and so are pieces when HeinOnline labels several in one volume "Note". A credit 30 or more years before a person's next credited article is left out, unless it is under the same spelling as their recent work. Wrong or missed matches: Corrections.

Journal ranks

  • Flagship law reviews take their school's rank (), and come in tiers of that rank ("1–3", "4–25", "26–50", "51 and above"); schools tied at a rank share a tier, and reviews of unranked schools are in "51 and above". The school rank can be the U.S. News overall rank (the default) or, with the "TaxProf peer rank" option (off by default), the peer-reputation rank from the U.S. News survey of deans and faculty, as published on TaxProf Blog. The default view counts the main law reviews of the top 25.
  • Specialty journals take their rank in the Washington & Lee Law Journal Rankings (, combined score). They sit in groups and higher-level groups (see below); in the filters, a higher-level group opens to its groups, and a group to its journals in order of W&L combined score, each with its score and the school that publishes it with that school's rank. "Count each higher-level group's top N" counts the N highest-scoring journals across the groups checked in it (journals W&L does not score count only when "all" is chosen), and "from schools with a U.S. News rank of" can limit the specialty journals to those published by schools in a rank range.

These ranks determine which journals are counted; schools are ordered by the count described above.

Default years

Twenty years of volumes, ending with the current year (now ), taken from the visitor's date when the page opens. Journals post their issues through the year, so the current year holds fewer articles until its volumes are in. An article's year is the last year in its volume's label (2024-2025 counts as 2025), except where that would put two consecutive volumes of a journal in one year and leave the year before empty; then the earlier volume takes the year before, inside its own label. A label that runs into next year counts as its first year. The "from" and "to" menus above the ranking change the years.

Comparison with U.S. News

Weights in the U.S. News overall rank: employment and bar passage 58%, admissions selectivity 10%, and the survey of deans and faculty 12.5%. This site counts articles by current faculty in the selected journals. Figures in this section were computed in October 2026 on one view: flagship law reviews of the U.S. News top 25 schools, 2017–2026 (ten years). Rank correlations (Spearman) over the top 50 schools: that view's order and the U.S. News overall rank, 0.81; that view's order and the U.S. News peer-reputation rank, 0.86; the overall rank and the peer-reputation rank, 0.86. Published studies of scholarship-based rankings report 0.62 to 0.80 (for example, Black & Caron, 81 Ind. L.J. 83 (2006)).

Rank correlation over the top 50 between a school's count and its number of faculty with an article in those journals and years: 0.95. Difference between a top-50 school's place here and its place in U.S. News, by component: size of its faculty, about 36%; share of its faculty with an article in these journals, 41%; articles per publishing professor, 23%. Rank correlations with U.S. News over the top 50 for the options: per professor, 0.70 to 0.80, depending on who counts as a professor; leaving out articles in a school's own law review, 0.78. Rate of publication in a host school's own law review, its faculty relative to other schools' faculty: one and a half to fourteen times; share of a host school's count from those articles: median 19.8%. Gaps in the data: some volumes are not collected yet (Data sources and dates), and a professor whose name is written two ways may be split (Corrections).

    Short pieces

    "Skip short pieces" leaves out a piece that runs under 10 pages, or a memorial or tribute under 20 pages (where the piece is identified as one; few are identified so far, and other memorials fall under the 10-page rule). The length comes from page numbers in HeinOnline's metadata: the next entry's first page minus this one's, counting each entry in the volume's table of contents (a volume's last piece has no next entry, so the classifier decides for it). Lengths are being added volume by volume (). Where a piece's length is not known yet, a trained classifier decides. It learns from the pieces whose length is measured (their titles, the journal, the piece's place in its volume, its co-authors and the titles around it) and is scored by stratified cross-validation on pieces it did not see: . So far: .

    A tribute, dedication, remembrance, roundtable or transcript signed by more than ten people also counts as a memorial piece, not a co-authored article. Table-of-contents entries with special handling: an article listed together with its responses ("... (with Responses by ...)") is credited to its own authors, not the respondents; an article continued in a second entry ("(cont.)") counts once; and a piece listed twice in one volume counts once only when it is the same entry (the same page in HeinOnline), not merely the same title on the same printed page of another issue.

    Examples (measured lengths):

    • Skipped: a dean's 2-page foreword; a 3-page reply to two other authors in a law review's online companion; a 6-page book review; a 2-page tribute to a judge's twenty years on the bench; 2-page opening remarks at a symposium.
    • Counted: a 12-page response essay; a 13-page essay in an online companion; a 20-page book review; full-length articles.

    Version numbers

    The volume number in the top left is a version, major.data.site. The middle number goes up when the substantive data changes (articles, faculty, schools, journals or their ranks; the site keeps a fingerprint of the data to tell), and the small code in the top right is the build: a short hash of the data and the site files together, which changes when either does. The last number goes up for changes to the site itself that leave the data alone. The first number goes up for a major update. The version history lists the versions.
