How these are computed

Who publishes where uses the rankings' own data. "Who publishes in a journal" shows every institution on the faculty lists (courts and non-US schools included); the rest of question 1 uses the articles of current faculty at U.S. law schools, credited to the school where each author teaches now. Article counts and the shares in the tiles and the row view count each article once (an article with authors from two groups of schools counts once in each group); the column view and the flows count it once for each school with an author on it. Flagship law reviews (each school's main law review) are placed by their school's rank, under the ranking chosen above (the U.S. News overall rank, or the TaxProf peer rank: the peer-reputation rank from the U.S. News survey of deans and faculty, as published on TaxProf Blog); schools tied at a rank sit together, and schools listed in a range ("175–194") at its top. With a school's own journals left out (an option in the bar above), an article does not count for a school that publishes its journal. "Online editions & other general journals" are the general journals that are not a school's main review; specialty journals are the rest (including two general journals that W&L also lists under a subject, the Journal of Legal Analysis and the University of Chicago Legal Forum, here and in question 2).

Co-authorship counts every piece in every journal collected here ( pieces), not only those by current faculty, so the trend is not an artifact of who teaches today. A piece's authors are the names HeinOnline lists for it. "Skip short pieces" (an option in the bar above) leaves out short pieces (under 10 pages by HeinOnline's page numbers, or a memorial under 20; where the length is not known, the short-piece classifier described on the About page decides). "Same journals throughout" keeps only the journals with articles in every five years since 1980, so a rise is not just newer journals joining. The latest year is partial until its volumes are complete.

Title length counts the characters of research-article titles from 1980 on, subtitle included. HeinOnline glues a section heading onto many titles with no separator ("… Note", "… Symposium: Law and the Internet"), so a title is measured only when that heading can be taken off for certain: the labels HeinOnline uses for sections (Note, Comment, Essay, Article, Symposium, Foreword, Recent Developments and the like) are removed, and a title is left out rather than cut at a guess when its last words also end two or more other titles in the same volume, or when any five words in a row of it appear in two or more other titles of the volume (a symposium's, a survey's or a panel's heading, wherever it sits). Short pieces and book reviews (the title is the book's) are left out too, and by the law review's rank, so are pieces HeinOnline labels as student work (Note, Comment, Casenote, Recent Case, Student Article and the like). By the law review's rank counts every such article in each school's main law review, whoever wrote it; by the author's school counts the articles of current faculty in every journal collected here, under the school where each author teaches now. Groups are by U.S. News overall rank whatever the rank option above, and short pieces are left out whatever the short-piece option; each point on the chart pools a year with the years either side, and the table gives single years.

Connections in the data uses the rankings' own data: articles (short pieces left out when that option is on) with at least one author on the faculty of a U.S. law school, from the years chosen in the bar above, each author credited to the school where they teach now. The subject-area shift compares the last ten of those years (all of them when there are fewer) with the same years two decades earlier, counting only today's faculty. Groups of schools are by the rank chosen above (U.S. News overall rank, or the TaxProf peer rank when that option is on) with ties kept (the top 14 is every school ranked 1st through 14th), and unranked schools join 101 & below. Two schools' shared articles count each article once for each pair of schools on it. The own-review chart counts each school's faculty's flagship-review articles and covers schools with their own review and at least 20 such articles. The co-authorship chart counts all of an article's authors, not only law faculty. The last chart counts an article once for each current faculty author on it. With a school's own journals left out, an article does not count for a school that publishes its journal (nor for that school's faculty in the last chart), and the own-review chart is not drawn.

How equal is publishing uses the rankings' own data: articles (short pieces left out when that option is on) in flagship law reviews, in the years chosen in the bar above, with an author on the faculty of a U.S. law school, each author at the school where they teach now; an article counts once for each school with an author on it. Reviews are grouped by their school's rank chosen above (U.S. News overall rank, or the TaxProf peer rank when that option is on), ties kept (the top 14 is every review whose school is ranked 1st through 14th). With a school's own journals left out, an article does not count for the school that publishes the review. Each Lorenz curve and Gini coefficient runs over every U.S. law school on the faculty lists, those with no articles included; the five-year windows end at the last year chosen and step back while they start within the chosen years (with all years, from 1980 on).

Anonymous review compares the flagship reviews that switched to anonymous (or partly anonymous) article selection in a known year with every other flagship review, volume year by volume year from 2008 on. The switch years come from the reviews' own submission pages and editors' statements, dated by archived copies of those pages; a review whose policy has no known start (Harvard, Yale and Stanford among them) cannot be tested before and after and is left out of the test. The articles are research articles (short pieces left out) with an author on the faculty of a U.S. law school, each author at the school where they teach now, so an older article is credited to its author's school today. The estimate is a difference in differences with review and year fixed effects, each review-year weighted by its articles, with standard errors clustered by review; because only a handful of reviews switched, a randomization test (the same switch years given to random sets of other top-100 reviews) is reported beside it. Every review's policy, with its source, is listed in the section. The years and options in the bar above do not change this section: its years and its counting are part of the test's design.

Words in titles uses the research-article titles of the title-length section, with the same rules for what is left out, from 1980 on, grouped into the same five tiers by the law review's school or by the school of a current faculty member credited with the title (U.S. News overall rank, with short pieces left out, whatever the options above). Each title is lowercased and cut into words (a hyphenated word stays one word, a possessive ’s is dropped); one-letter words, words with no letter, the 179 words of NLTK's standard English stopword list and words that name a piece's genre or place in an issue (law, legal, essay, article, note, comment, introduction, foreword, afterword, symposium, response, reply, lecture, remarks and the like) are dropped, and two-word phrases are pairs of kept words next to each other with no punctuation between them; a title counts each word or phrase once. The tier-against-tiers and window-against-window comparisons are scored with the weighted log-odds ratio with an informative Dirichlet prior of Monroe, Colaresi and Quinn (2008, “Fightin’ Words”), the prior for each word being its count in the two groups together, reported as a z-score (the log-odds ratio over its standard error, not adjusted for the number of words compared); the prior is as large as the two groups' own counts, which draws a smaller group's rates toward the two groups' combined rate, so the z-scores order the words within one comparison and are on different scales for comparisons of different sizes. Listed are words in at least 25 titles of the two groups together and in 10 titles and 3 journals of the side they are listed for, with no more than half of those titles in one journal; a word is not listed beside a two-word phrase that is in 80% or more of its titles. By the author's school, the earlier window holds only the titles of authors on a law faculty today, under the school where each teaches now. Title form is the share of titles with a colon or a first-person word, or opening with one of the listed phrases, and the mean words per title and characters per word, per five-year window; HeinOnline's metadata carries question marks and double quotation marks in titles from about 2020 on and seldom before, so those two are listed for the latest window only. Term families are fixed word lists, printed with the chart, and a title counts when it has any of a family's words. A window with fewer than 30 titles in a tier is left blank.

The numbers behind every chart are in data/data.json and data/insights.json on GitHub. The charts are drawn with Highcharts.
